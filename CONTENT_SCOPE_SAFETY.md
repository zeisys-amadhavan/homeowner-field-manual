# Content Scope and Safety

## Scope boundary

The book teaches homeowners to:

- recognize systems;
- understand basic function;
- locate important components;
- document their own house;
- identify abnormal conditions;
- communicate effectively with professionals;
- perform ordinary homeowner-level actions where appropriate.

It is not intended to become a comprehensive repair manual.

## Safe-DIY principle

The book should make the boundary between:

- observation;
- ordinary homeowner action;
- work requiring qualified professional help

clear enough to prevent confidence from outrunning competence.

## Safety writing

Warnings should be specific to the actual hazard.

Avoid generic warning clutter.

A safety note should normally tell the reader one or more of:

- what condition is dangerous;
- what not to touch, open, move, energize, disconnect, or enter;
- when to shut something down;
- when to leave the area;
- when to call an appropriate professional or emergency service.

## High-risk subjects

Exercise particular restraint around:

- energized electrical equipment;
- fuel gas;
- combustion and carbon monoxide;
- structural failure;
- roofs and fall hazards;
- pressurized systems;
- sewage;
- fire;
- hazardous materials;
- major water intrusion;
- equipment requiring licensed or specialized work.

## National/general-purpose default

The book is intended for broad U.S. homeowner use rather than one locality.

Do not present a local code requirement, climate assumption, utility practice, insurance rule, or permitting process as universally applicable.

When jurisdiction matters, instruct the homeowner to verify locally.

## Not a code book

Codes and standards may help explain why something exists, but the book should not attempt to reproduce or replace current building, electrical, plumbing, mechanical, fire, zoning, or other regulatory codes.

## Not a professional substitute

The book improves the homeowner's ability to understand and document their house.

It does not substitute for inspection, engineering, legal, insurance, tax, trade, or other professional advice where those services are appropriate.
