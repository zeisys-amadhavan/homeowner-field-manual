# Writing Rules

## Primary writing objective

Write so a capable homeowner can understand the concept quickly, recognize it in their own house, and know what information matters enough to record.

The book should teach **useful recognition and judgment**, not reward technical vocabulary.

## Reading level

Prefer clear, direct language understandable by a general homeowner.

Use the simplest technically correct word.

Introduce specialist terminology only when the homeowner is likely to encounter that term on equipment, documents, estimates, inspections, or conversations with professionals.

## Writing hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. visual explanation;
2. short labels and callouts;
3. concise explanatory copy;
4. longer prose only when genuinely necessary.

Do not turn a diagram page into an essay.

## Teach by observation

Whenever possible, make the homeowner learn by inspecting their own property.

Good instructions cause actions such as:

- find it;
- locate it;
- count it;
- read the label;
- identify the material;
- record the date;
- photograph it;
- compare it;
- verify where the document is stored.

## Write for the homeowner, not the trade

Organize and explain concepts according to how a homeowner encounters the house.

Where professional taxonomy and homeowner intuition conflict, homeowner usability generally wins unless safety or accuracy requires otherwise.

## Tone

The voice should be:

- confident;
- practical;
- calm;
- concise;
- non-patronizing;
- visually oriented.

Avoid:

- marketing fluff inside instructional pages;
- unnecessary jokes;
- fear-based language;
- jargon for its own sake;
- long introductions before useful information;
- repeating the same idea in multiple callouts.

## Safety language

Warnings should identify a meaningful boundary or consequence, not merely add generic caution.

Tell the reader when observation or simple user-level action ends and professional work begins.

See `CONTENT_SCOPE_SAFETY.md`.

## Form-adjacent writing

Guide copy should help the reader successfully complete the paired form.

If a fact appears as a form field, the guide should explain enough for a normal homeowner to identify that fact correctly.

## Page test

Every educational page should earn its space through a useful insight, decision, recognition skill, or visual explanation.

If a passage can be removed without changing what the homeowner understands or records, cut it.
