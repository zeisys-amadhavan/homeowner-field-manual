# Project Definition

## One-sentence definition

**The Homeowner's Field Manual** is a printed field manual that teaches homeowners how the systems of their house work while guiding them to document their own house, so the completed book becomes the operating record the house never came with.

## The problem

Homeowners inherit a complex physical, legal, financial, and operational asset with no unified manual.

Two gaps result:

- **Knowledge gap:** the owner does not know how a system works, what is normal, what is urgent, or where safe DIY ends.
- **Record gap:** critical facts such as model numbers, installation dates, shutoff locations, service history, coverage limits, warranties, contractors, and property records are scattered or missing.

Most products solve only one side: teaching or recordkeeping. This book deliberately combines both.

## Primary objectives

1. **Teach by observation.** Completing the book should require the owner to physically inspect and understand their own house.
2. **Create a document of truth.** A completed copy should answer real questions from homeowners, contractors, inspectors, adjusters, buyers, and future owners.
3. **Cover the whole ownership system.** Land, structure, utilities, safety, records, insurance, technology, and life inside the home all belong in one coherent system.
4. **Remain useful for years.** The book should become more valuable as information accumulates.
5. **Make the homeowner a competent customer.** The reader should be able to describe a system or symptom clearly, find relevant records, and communicate intelligently with professionals.
6. **Survive the handoff.** The completed manual should be useful to the next owner or person responsible for the property.
7. **Be giftable.** The product should work naturally as a closing, housewarming, realtor, builder, or family gift.

## Product model

The book is a **hybrid visual guide + working record**.

Teaching without documentation becomes a reference book.
Documentation without teaching becomes a logbook.

The combination is the product.

## Core instructional principle

**The documentation task is part of the teaching method.**

To fill in a page, the homeowner may need to find a shutoff, read a nameplate, count panel spaces, locate a survey marker, inspect a policy declaration, or identify a component. The act of recording the house teaches the house.

## Positioning

Do not position the product primarily as a maintenance logbook or blank homeowner journal.

Position it as a **field manual for the house you own**: a visual guide that teaches the owner what they are looking at and gives them a standardized place to record their own house.

## Primary audience

The primary reader is a capable homeowner who wants to understand and organize their house but has never been systematically taught how ownership and home systems fit together.

Important secondary audiences include:

- newly settled homeowners;
- long-time owners who never documented the property;
- sellers preparing records;
- people inheriting or taking responsibility for a house;
- realtors, builders, parents, and others buying the book as a gift.

## Core value test

A page should normally pass at least one of these tests:

### Aha Test
Does the guide page teach a useful idea a normal homeowner may not already understand?

### Spec Sheet Test
Will the completed form answer a real future question about this specific house?

Pages that pass neither test should be challenged.

## Non-goals

The book is:

- not a comprehensive repair manual;
- not a building-code reference;
- not a physics or engineering textbook;
- not a survival/prepper manual;
- not a storage binder for complete policies, warranties, permits, or other large documents;
- not dependent on an app or online service;
- not region-specific by default.

The book may teach enough to recognize, describe, locate, document, monitor, and safely respond to a system without attempting to replace qualified professionals.
