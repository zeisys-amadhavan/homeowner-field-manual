# Form Rules

## Purpose

A form page records the homeowner's actual house.

A completed form should function as a concise, usable specification or record rather than a journal entry.

## Absolute placement

Forms belong on **even / left pages**.

See `PAGE_SPREAD_RULES.md`.

## Core test

A form should pass the **Spec Sheet Test**:

> Years from now, can the homeowner or a professional answer a real question by looking at or photographing this page?

If not, challenge the field or the form.

## Field selection

Record information that is likely to matter for:

- identification;
- location;
- operation;
- service;
- replacement;
- warranty;
- insurance;
- contractors;
- future ownership;
- troubleshooting communication;
- legal or financial reference.

Do not collect information merely because it exists.

## Fillability

Forms must be designed for actual handwriting.

Provide enough room for realistic answers.

Do not compress a form until it technically fits while becoming unpleasant to complete.

The current 8 × 10 format should be used to improve writing space and table usability.

## Field labels

Use short labels.

Where a term may be unfamiliar, the paired guide page should teach the homeowner how to identify it.

Avoid embedding paragraphs of instructions inside the form.

## Structure

Prefer:

- checkboxes for discrete choices;
- short blanks for identifiers;
- tables for repeated assets;
- dates in consistent formats;
- clearly separated location fields;
- generous notes space only where variable conditions genuinely require it.

## Dating and currency

Every form carries a date field recording when it was last updated.

A form without a date cannot be trusted years later, because the reader cannot tell whether it describes the current house or a previous condition.

## Keeping a form current

Systems get replaced, and a form that was accurate on the day it was filled in will eventually describe equipment that no longer exists.

The book handles this by treating superseded entries as history rather than error:

- The reader strikes through the outdated entry rather than erasing it, and dates the strike.
- The replacement is recorded in the space provided, or on a fresh copy of the form.
- Where a form has room for only one generation of a system, it should provide a short replacement history line so the sequence survives.

The design consequence: **a form should be legible after it has been corrected.** Do not design layouts so tight that a struck-through line destroys the page.

The completed book is a record of system generations, not a snapshot.

## Paste-in and photo areas

Some forms benefit from a physical attachment — a photograph, a paint chip, a label image, a fragment of a document.

Where a form provides one:

- The area is explicitly drawn with corner marks, not implied by blank space.
- Its usable dimensions are stated on the page.
- The surrounding fields remain complete without the attachment, so an unfilled paste-in area does not create a gap in the record.

Do not add paste-in areas by default. They are for cases where an image carries information no field can.

## Records versus storage

The book records standardized summaries and references.

It does not attempt to physically contain complete policies, warranties, deeds, permits, inspection reports, or manuals.

Forms may record where those source documents are stored.

## Sharing

Forms should remain understandable when photographed and shared with a contractor, inspector, adjuster, buyer, or other professional.

The page code should remain visible in that context.

## Source of truth

Exact fields for each form belong only in the `Form Page Fields` column of `toc.csv`.

Do not duplicate page-specific field lists here.
